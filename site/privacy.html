Privacy

Last updated 29 August 2026

Shopping List is software you run, not a service we operate. That single fact decides almost everything below.

The short version. Until you enter a server address the app sends nothing anywhere — your lists are a file on your device. Once you enter one, the apps talk to that server and nowhere else. Nothing is sent to the makers of this app, there is no analytics of any kind, and this website makes no third-party requests and sets no cookies.

Who is responsible for your data

Your lists live on a server that somebody runs — unless you have not set one up, in which case they live only on your device and nobody else holds them at all. Where there is a server, under UK and EU data protection law the person running it is the controller of everything on it.

  • If you use no server, there is no controller but you. The lists are in a file inside the app on your device, they are not sent anywhere, and they go when the app does.
  • If you run the server, you are the controller. The data is in a single SQLite file on your machine, and nobody else — including us — can reach it.
  • If somebody invited you onto their server, they are the controller. Ask them about retention, deletion and backups; those are their decisions to make, not ours.

We publish the apps. We do not operate a server, receive a copy of your data, or hold keys to anybody else's.

What the apps send, and where

With no server configured, nothing leaves the device. The app runs the server's own code locally against a file of its own, which is why it is fully featured offline rather than a reduced version of itself.

When you enter a server address it is stored in the app's settings, where you can change it later, and all requests go to that address and nowhere else. There is no fallback server, no default endpoint and no “phone home” on launch.

There is one endpoint you can add yourself: under Settings → Metrics the app can report its own timings — how long requests take, how deep the queue is, how often syncing fails — to an OpenTelemetry collector you name. It is empty by default, nothing is sent until you fill it in, the field is offered only when a server is configured, and nothing about the contents of your lists is included.

Signing in

Signing in happens only if you use a server. On iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple Watch it is Sign in with Apple; on Android and in a web browser it is Google Sign-In. Either way the provider verifies who you are and issues a token that the app passes to your server, and the app never sees or stores a password. Your use of them is covered by Apple's privacy policy and Google's privacy policy respectively.

From that token the server records the account identifier the provider issued, and your email address and display name where the provider supplies them. The email address is what a server operator uses to decide who may sign in. Apple lets you hide your real address, and a relay address works here exactly like any other.

What the server stores

  • Your lists, the items on them, their amounts, units and categories
  • Notes you attach to a list
  • A private history of what you have bought before, which is what makes suggestions work. History is per person: two people sharing a list do not merge habits.
  • Share invitations, stored only as a hash of the link, never the link itself
  • Browser sessions, if you use the web interface

All of it is in the server's database file. Its retention and backup are the operator's business.

What stays on your device

  • With no server: your lists themselves, in a database file inside the app
  • The server address you entered, if you entered one
  • Your sign-in credential, held by the operating system's keychain or credential manager — not by this app
  • A copy of the lists and items last loaded, so the app works with no connection, together with any changes waiting to go up
  • A diagnostic log, only if you turn one on — see below

Signing out or changing the server address clears the cached copy.

Diagnostic logs

Logging is off by default, and even off the app records its own failures so that a report is worth reading. At that setting, and at Basic, the log contains no list names and nothing on them.

Detailed and Everything do record the contents of your lists. The app warns you before it will turn either on. The log never leaves the device on its own — it is written to a file you can delete, and it goes somewhere else only when you export it and send it to somebody. If you do, you are sending them your shopping.

What we do not do

  • No analytics, no usage tracking, no crash reporting
  • No advertising, and no advertising identifiers
  • No selling or sharing of personal data, and nothing that would count as “sharing” for targeted advertising under any privacy law
  • No third-party SDK other than Google Sign-In on Android and in the browser. Sign in with Apple is part of the operating system.

This website

These pages are static files. No cookies are set, no analytics run, and nothing is loaded from another domain — the type you are reading is the one already on your device.

Your rights

You have the right to ask for a copy of your data, to have it corrected, and to have it deleted. Because the data sits on somebody's own server, those requests go to whoever runs it. Closing an account removes that person's lists, items, notes and history along with it.

If you run the server yourself, you can act on all of this directly: the database is one file, and you own the machine it is on.

Children

The app is not directed at children and collects nothing beyond what is described above from anybody.
